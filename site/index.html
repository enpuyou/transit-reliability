Transit pipeline transparency

How real-time King County Metro bus data becomes reliability numbers, one step at a time: what goes in, what each stage does to it, how the data looks, and how we know it is working. Everything below is read from the pipeline; nothing is typed in by hand except the explanations. This page is not real time: the charts and tables are rebuilt hourly, and a lighter set of live probes (is the collector still delivering? did the jobs run?) is refreshed about every 15 minutes. The panel below says how old each is and tells a pipeline problem apart from a page that has stopped updating. What these numbers can and cannot tell you

  • Arrival times are estimates. Buses report GPS positions every 20 seconds or so. We work out when a bus reached a stop by placing those positions along the route, and we always keep the interval between the last position before the stop and the first one after.
  • Not every stop is measured. About two thirds of timepoints (stops with a published time) have a measured arrival. The first stop of a trip is almost never measured, because the bus is often first seen after it has left; the last stop only occasionally. Percentages describe the middle of trips.
  • "Early" is judged on arrival. Buses are supposed to wait at timepoints until the scheduled time, so arriving early is normal; leaving early is the real problem.
  • The feed's "next stop" pointer moves early, and we do not trust it over the GPS. About one arrival in ten, the agency advances its pointer a little before the bus reaches the stop (typically around 75 feet short). When that conflicts with the GPS position we keep the GPS interval and record how short of the stop the bus was. Until 2026-10-20 such arrivals were wrongly shown as exact to the second; that was found by the independent review process described below and fixed.
  • Partial days. Collection began on 2026-10-13 about 21:43 UTC and the first and latest days are incomplete; their missing trips are missing data, not missed service.
  • Independent review. A separate reviewer with no knowledge of how the pipeline was built audited the code and the data on 2026-10-19 and found a real bug, which was fixed and the data rebuilt.
