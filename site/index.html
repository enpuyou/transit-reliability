The agency never tells us "the bus reached this stop at 4:25". We work it out from the bus's GPS reports, using the timetable's map of the route. Each square is one run of a scheduled job over the last 7 days (green succeeded, red failed). A gap in a row means the job did not run when it should have. Gaps are the failures a job cannot report about itself, which is why a separate watchdog outside Databricks checks them.

What these numbers can and cannot tell you

  • Arrival times are estimates. Buses report GPS positions every 20 seconds or so. We work out when a bus reached a stop by placing those positions along the route, and we always keep the interval between the last position before the stop and the first one after.
  • The headline is a definition, not a measurement of "good service". The rider rule counts a bus as early only if it left early and as late only if it arrived late (at the first stop, if it left late). That is deliberately kinder than judging arrivals alone, because a bus that arrives early and waits is normal. Its on-time share is therefore higher than the arrival-only view shown beside it; the difference comes from the definition, not from better service.
  • The last stop of each trip is not scored. A trip's position data usually ends about 1,000 feet before the final stop, and the bus then drops out of the feed for several minutes, so its arrival there is almost never measured and it has no departure. About nine in ten of the remaining timepoints can be scored.
  • Departures are less certain than they look. Sometimes the last thing we saw was the bus still standing at a stop. Then we know it left no earlier than that moment but not when; this is used as evidence of lateness or "not early" and can never alone prove a bus on time.
  • The feed's "next stop" pointer moves early, and we do not trust it over the GPS. About one arrival in ten, the agency advances its pointer a little before the bus reaches the stop (typically around 75 feet short). When that conflicts with the GPS position we keep the GPS interval and record how short of the stop the bus was. Until 2026-10-20 such arrivals were wrongly shown as exact to the second; that was found by the independent review process described below and fixed.
  • Partial days. Collection began on 2026-10-13 at about 2:43 pm Seattle time and the first and latest days are incomplete; their missing trips are missing data, not missed service.
  • Independent review. A separate reviewer with no knowledge of how the pipeline was built audited the code and the data on 2026-10-19 and found a real bug, which was fixed and the data rebuilt.
